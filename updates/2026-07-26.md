| # | File(s) | Bug | Severity |
|---|---------|-----|----------|
| 18 | `models/chronos2/finetune.py` (`KNOWN_FUTURE_COLS`), `Processing/ablation.py` (`COVARIATE_GROUPS["events"]`) | The pipeline has two differently-named event-proximity signals that never got reconciled. `machine_learning_files/merge_pipeline.py`'s `compute_event_features` computes `hours_to_event` (real, populated — count of home games/events that calendar month; `zero_shot.py`'s `past_covariate_cols` already uses this one directly). `Processing/feature_engineering.py`'s `add_event_features` always takes its `_add_monthly_event_features` fallback at this pipeline's monthly cadence, which explicitly sets `hours_to_next_event`/`hours_since_last_event` to `np.nan` (hour-scale concepts with no monthly equivalent, per its own comment). `KNOWN_FUTURE_COLS` and ablation's `COVARIATE_GROUPS["events"]` both listed `hours_to_next_event` — the always-NaN column — while never referencing `hours_to_event`, the one that actually carries signal. AutoGluon's known-covariate validation typically rejects all-NaN columns, so `finetune.py`'s `predictor.fit(...)` was at risk of raising outright, or at best silently wasting a covariate slot on dead data — either way the fine-tuned/ensemble model never saw the real event-count signal the zero-shot path does use, and the ablation study never exercised it either. Fixed by swapping `hours_to_next_event` → `hours_to_event` in both `KNOWN_FUTURE_COLS` and `COVARIATE_GROUPS["events"]` (dropping the always-NaN `hours_since_last_event` entry from the ablation group too, since it has no monthly analog). | High |
