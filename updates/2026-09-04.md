## Considered but not changed

- `machine_learning_files/merge_pipeline.py`'s `agg_kwargs` computes
  `is_raining=("is_raining", "mean")`, producing a per-station-month
  **fraction** (0.0–1.0) in the raw `feature_store.parquet`, contradicting
  that file's own `Schema:` docstring (`is_raining bool`). This is exactly
  the root cause flagged in passing on 2026-08-14 ("flagged for a future
  pass") but never fixed. In the **properly-run pipeline**, this is
  harmless: `feature_engineering.py`'s `add_weather_features()`
  unconditionally recomputes `is_raining = precip_mm > 0.1` (a real bool)
  when building `feature_store_enriched.parquet`, and today's fix #1 above
  makes `ablation.py` pull `is_raining` from that enriched store rather
  than the raw one. The remaining exposure is `machine_learning_files/
  zero_shot.py`'s `load_feature_store()`, which loads the **raw**
  `feature_store.parquet` directly and feeds `is_raining` to Chronos as a
  covariate per its own `WEATHER_COLS` list — a continuous 0–1 signal
  where a boolean is documented/expected. Not a crash (Chronos accepts any
  numeric covariate) and not clearly wrong (arguably a *more* informative
  signal than a hard threshold), so left as a documented type mismatch
  rather than a unilateral redesign of the aggregation.
- `machine_learning_files/merge_pipeline.py`'s `add_calendar_features` and
  `Processing/feature_engineering.py`'s `add_time_features` both compute
  `is_holiday`/`is_holiday_eve` via an exact-date match (`ts.dt.date.map(...)`)
  against timestamps that, at the pipeline's real `"MS"` (month-start)
  cadence, are always the 1st of the month at midnight. Verified over
  2019–2030: `is_holiday` is `True` for only 10 of 144 months (basically
  only some January 1sts) and `is_holiday_eve` for only 4 of 144 — both are
  listed as known-future covariates in `finetune.py`'s `KNOWN_FUTURE_COLS`
  and `ablation.py`'s `COVARIATE_GROUPS["calendar"]`, so the model trains
  on a "holiday" signal that's silently ~93–97% noise at production
  cadence. Unlike `is_am_peak`/`is_pm_peak` (explicitly guarded off at
  monthly cadence in both files, precisely because they'd otherwise be
  fully constant) and the event-proximity features (already adapted to a
  monthly-count proxy), this received no equivalent treatment. Not fixed:
  redefining it (e.g. "does this month contain a federal holiday") is a
  feature-design decision, not a bug fix with one obviously-correct
  answer, and both call sites would need the same redefinition kept in
  sync — flagging for the project owner rather than picking unilaterally.
